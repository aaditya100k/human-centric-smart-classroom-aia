5. Engagement Labeling Procedure

Kappa = 0.84

Operationalized through a 4-tier index annotated by 3 independent educational psychologists at 5-second synchronized clip intervals:

Highly Engaged (85-100%) Sustained forward gaze, active note-taking, positive valence
Nominally Engaged (65-84%) Occasional downward glance, steady upright posture, neutral
Passively Disengaged (40-64%) Horizontal head turn, off-board gaze, resting face
Severely Distracted (<40%) Head on desk, smartphone posture, eye closure > 400ms

Inter-Rater Reliability: Cohen's Kappa κ = 0.84 achieved across annotators on 200 calibration validation clips.

6. Emotion Labeling Procedure

FACS Action Units

Ground-truth expression mapping follows the Facial Action Coding System (FACS) across 6 operationalized affective states:

Focused AU4 (brow furrow) + AU7 (lid tighten) + forward eye fixations
Happy AU6 (cheek raiser) + AU12 (lip corner puller) - Duchenne smile
Stressed AU1 + AU4 (brow raise/knit) + AU20 (lip stretch), rapid saccades
Bored / Drowsy AU43 (eye closure > 400ms) + AU26/27 (jaw drop/yawn)
Neutral Resting facial baseline tone without sustained AU activation

Temporal Smoothing: 5-frame sliding window majority voting suppresses optical micro-flicker and involuntary ocular blinks.

shield Why Random Frame Splitting is Scientifically Invalid in Classroom Biometrics:

If frames are split randomly across video clips (as seen in flawed naive ML pipelines), multiple consecutive frames of student Alex appear in both the training set and the test set. Under this identity leakage, neural networks do not learn generalized emotion or robust facial feature embeddings; instead, they simply memorize Alex's facial morphology, bone structure, skin tone, glasses, and haircut. This results in misleadingly inflated accuracy numbers (>98%) that catastrophically fail when a new student steps in front of the classroom camera.

By enforcing subject-wise student-independent partitioning (Stratified Group K-Fold by unique Student ID), our test partition guarantees that the model has never observed the student's face before. The resulting 91.4% accuracy is a genuine measurement of zero-shot out-of-sample identity verification generalization.
